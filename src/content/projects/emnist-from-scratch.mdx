## Problem

Most ML engineers treat PyTorch as a black box. I wanted to prove I understand
forward pass, backprop, gradient descent, and overfitting mechanics at the
matrix level — no autograd.

## Approach

1. **From scratch NumPy implementation:** Softmax regression, logistic
   regression, shallow (1 hidden layer) networks, and deep (3+ hidden layer)
   architectures — all using NumPy only.

2. **EMNIST letters dataset:** 85,800 samples across 26 letter classes (balanced
   subset). Standard 70/15/15 train/val/test split.

3. **Overfitting analysis:** Compared shallow vs deep architectures on
   train vs validation curves. Documented exactly when each architecture starts
   to overfit and which regularisation (L2, dropout, early stopping) worked
   best.

## Result

- **All 4 architectures implemented in NumPy** — proves I can hand derive
  gradients.
- **Confusion matrix error analysis** as a separate artefact.
- **Reproducibility:** `requirements.lock.txt` pins NumPy version; Dockerfile
  runs the entire pipeline in 4 commands.

## Why this matters

Hiring managers consistently say "show me fundamentals" before "show me
frameworks". This project is a strong interview talking point and signals
that the candidate will not panic when PyTorch misbehaves in production.

Especially relevant for ML platform teams where deep bugs often hide at
the framework boundary.
